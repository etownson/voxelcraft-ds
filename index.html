      The basics

      Your mission. You were teleported here to relight five beacons. Each one you light sends a transmission: a little more of the story, new recipes, and the way to the next beacon. The Start page shows what the next one wants.

      Getting started. You arrive empty-handed. Punch a tree for logs. Beacon 1 stands a short walk away, and you carry just enough essence for it: right-click the dark monument to light it and receive your first recipes.

      Essence. Hostile creatures leave essence bubbles where they die. Run through them before they fade, or they are lost. Mining coal, iron and diamond ore pays a little too. Every later beacon, and enchanting, is paid in essence.

      Mining stone and ore requires a pickaxe with a minimum strength. A pickaxe that is too weak breaks the block slowly and gives nothing. Minimum pickaxe requirements: stone=wood, coal=wood, iron=stone, diamond=iron, and obsidian=diamond.

      Finding your way. The arrow on screen points to the next beacon. On the Waypoints page you can save places of your own and have the arrow follow them, or a friend in co-op.

      Staying alive. You have ten hearts and a food bar. Health only comes back while you are well fed, and an empty stomach hurts. Night and dark caves bring zombies and creepers; torchlight keeps an area safe. A bed sets your respawn point and sleeps the night away. You keep your inventory when you die.

      Danger grows outward. The farther you travel from where you first arrived, the tougher enemies get. The pips on screen show the zone you stand in: green is behind your last beacon, yellow is its zone, and red is beyond what your recipes were made for.

      Not quite Minecraft

      Recipes are earned. Nothing can be crafted until a beacon teaches it. The journal (J) lists every recipe you have received, ready to use.

      One long journey. After the first, each beacon stands about half a day’s walk past the one before it. Plan for a night on the road.

      Enemies scale with distance, not with a difficulty setting, and new kinds join as the mission goes on: spiders after beacon 2, skeleton archers after beacon 3.

      Food is harder to come by. Raw meat must be cooked in a furnace before it feeds you, while wheat can be eaten as it is. Grass rarely gives seeds, apples fall from leaves more often, and there are no saplings. Animals do not breed, so a wheat farm is your steady food.

      Enchanting costs essence, not experience. Each item can be enchanted once, and after that repaired at the table for the same price.

      Swords and armour hit and hold harder than Minecraft’s numbers, so each step up is clearly felt.

      Creeper blasts keep what they break. Every block a blast destroys drops as an item, and a wall between you and the creeper shields you.

      Sprint is a toggle. Press it once to run and again to stop.

      Co-op shares the mission. Host from the Co-op page and send the link; up to four players. Essence goes into one purse for the whole room.
