# MultiVoxelVerse Adventures  
  
You spawn into a new world. Your initial spawn is a scene highlighted with graphics and sound to designate the importance of the event.   
  
A chat bubble appears that is a transmission from a distant place. All transmissions are understandable but a bit spotty in places. The following comes through:  
- The transmitter tells you that you have been teleported here to fully reactivate the beacons previously placed in the world  
- The transmitter knows you are disoriented from the teleportation process.  
- The transmitter tells you that you need to find and activate a beacon that should be nearby.  
- The transmitter tells you that your mission is urgent but not enough of the transmission comes through to understand why.   
  
You start with no items in your inventory. You can punch trees and gather logs like Minecraft but no crafting has been unlocked because it is gated by the requirement to find recipes. You receive new recipes by finding and activating beacons. Activating a beacon requires essence. You start with just enough essence to do one beacon activation. The recipes you have received are available in a journal. You do not have to do anything further to unlock the recipes. Once you receive them are then ready to be used.  
  
The initial point where you spawn into the world is spawn point 0 (SP0). The first beacon is near SP0 but is just far enough away to require a short bit of exploration.  
  
Enemy difficulty scales based on their distance along the X and Z axes from where they initially spawn versus SP0. Enemies only start additionally scaling in difficulty along the Y axis once they are below sea level. Enemy scaling dimensions are spawn frequency, health, and attack damage. The world divides into threat zones, SP0’s and one around each beacon, each reaching halfway to its neighbours. The HUD shows the zone underfoot as pips, coloured green behind the most recently lit beacon’s zone, yellow in it, and red beyond it, so the walker can tell when they have outrun what their recipes were meant for.  
  
You earn essence by killing enemies. The amount of essence received scales with the enemy difficulty. Enemies drop essence bubbles within a close random distance near the area where they die. You must pick them up by running over them (intersecting with them). Otherwise they fade out and are lost. They take a short time to appear so that the player has to be more conscious about navigating to pick them while in the heat of battle.  
  
The first beacon costs what the walker starts with, one essence per 1.5 blocks from SP0. Every later beacon costs about thirty kills’ worth of essence, at the yield an enemy pays on the ground of the beacon lit before it, so each new beacon asks for the same hunt in the zone the last one opened.  
  
Each time you activate a new beacon you receive a unique transmission with the following:  
- A little more of the story narrative.  
- Some new recipes that help you survive well enough in the outer areas necessary to reach the next beacon.  
- The way to the next beacon. The HUD's waypoint arrow points at it from the moment the last one is lit, and the main menu's mission page says what it wants and what you carry. No coordinates are shown: the arrow is the way. The walker can also save places of their own under a name and have the arrow follow those, or any beacon already lit.  Note: The current game’s world generation will need to be adjusted so that the first two beacon positions are created after the initial part of the world is generated such that they make sense. For example, the beacon position cannot be within a body of water.

## Beacon Number 1 Transmission  
You understand the following from the transmission:  
- You are being given recipes to help you survive.   
- You are now incarnated as a biological creature and the transmitter apologizes for subjecting you to the horrors of dealing with health and hunger.  
- The transmitter warns about awful creatures at night but urges you to be brave and extract essence from them.  
- The transmitter expresses urgency for you to find the second beacon but warns you to first establish a base before attempting to activate it.  
- The transmission closes by urging you to prepare for nightfall: torches lit, a door hung and food cooked before the sun goes down.  
  
The initial transmission provides enough recipes so that a player can survive in the area around SP0, establish a base, and ultimately make it to the second beacon for further unlocks.

## Archery and the night's newcomers  
The night grows with the mission. Before beacon 2 a hostile spawn is a creeper 16% of the time and a zombie otherwise. Once beacon 2 is lit, a fifth of spawns roll a spider. A spider that would not fit the spot arrives as a zombie instead, so about one spawn in ten is a spider. Once beacon 3 is lit, another fifth of spawns are skeletons. The newcomers take their share out of the zombies'. All of them scale with the ground they were spawned on, as every enemy does, and pay essence when killed.  
  
**Spiders** (beacon 2). 16 health, quick, wide and low: 1.28 blocks across, wider than one block. A spider climbs any wall it runs into while it walks. It bites for 2 on a one-second cooldown. Sunlight does not burn it. Out under the daytime sky it hunts nobody unless it is hit, and then it stays angry for twelve seconds. In a dark cave it hunts as it would at night. It drops 0–2 string.  
  
**Skeleton archers** (beacon 3). 20 health. Within 24 blocks a skeleton holds its distance instead of closing it: it walks in from beyond 13 blocks, backs away inside 6, and in between circles the walker, changing side every few seconds. With a clear line of sight within 18 blocks it looses an arrow about every 2.2 seconds. The arrow aims high by the drop its flight will take and wobbles a little, and hits for 3 before difficulty scaling. Skeletons burn in sunlight like zombies. A skeleton drops 0–2 arrows, and its arrows that miss stick where they land and are gone after four seconds.  
  
**The bow.** Hold the use button (right click, or the left trigger) to draw, and let go to shoot. A full draw takes a second. A full draw shoots an arrow at 42 blocks a second for 7 damage, and a partial draw shoots slower and weaker in proportion. A draw under a tenth of the way lets go without shooting. Each shot spends one arrow from anywhere in the pack and one of the bow's 384 uses. Without arrows the bow says so. Arrows fall under gravity. The walker's arrows that miss land as an arrow item to pick up again, and one that hits is spent. The bow mines nothing and swings in melee like any tierless tool. Without pointer lock the right button is the look drag, so a right click there shoots a full draw at once.

## The Recipe Book  
The journal's first page draws each received recipe instead of listing it:  
- A card for each thing that can be made, shelved under the beacon that sent it (Beacon 1, survival kit; Beacon 2, farming, building and stone; Beacon 3, iron; Beacon 4, diamond). Recipes that make the same thing share one card and show each way to make it in turn.  
- The highlighted card is laid out on its real grid: the 2×2 pocket grid or the table's 3×3, then the result with its count, whether it needs the crafting table, and what it uses.  
- Once the furnace recipe has arrived, the book also shows what the furnace turns into what.  
- A beacon that has not been lit gets one dim line saying how many recipes are waiting there. It does not show what they are, so the book never spoils a beacon.  
- The book reads the same in the main menu and in the world, and the keyboard, the mouse and a controller can all reach every card.  
  
—  
## Journal Of Previous Transmissions  
The transmissions are spotty on purpose, so a line is easy to miss the first time it comes through. The journal keeps them:  
- Every journal has two pages, Recipes and Transmissions. (This was once a setting that was off by default; hiding the way back to a missed line behind a switch read as stingy.)  
- The Transmissions page holds every transmission received so far. Each entry is headed by the beacon that carried it: Arrival at SP0, then Beacon 1, Beacon 2, and so on. Entries run oldest first, so the story reads in the order it was heard.  
- Reading the journal is quiet. It carries the words only, with no sound and no line by line timing, and it never replays the transmission. The panel that talks over the hotbar still fires once, at the moment the beacon is lit.  
- Nothing arrives early. An entry joins the journal only when its transmission is received, so the journal cannot spoil a beacon that has not been lit.  
- The journal asks for no saving of its own. The text is the same in every world, so the count of lit beacons the save already keeps is enough to know which entries the player has heard, the arrival transmission included.
